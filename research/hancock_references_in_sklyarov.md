# Hancock Divergence Reference: Graham Hancock in Sklyarov's Works

**Document scope:** All 32 per-book extraction files (01–32), sections 2.2, 2.5, 2.6, 2.7, 2.8.
**Prepared:** 2026-03-20
**Method:** Exhaustive grep across all numbered extraction files for "Hancock," "Хэнкок," "Fingerprints," "Отпечатки," "Schoch," "Шох," "Younger Dryas," "ayahuasca," and related terms; full context reading of every hit.

---

## Summary

Graham Hancock appears by name in **10 of the 32 books** (Books 03, 04, 06, 08, 10, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 22, 23, 24). The primary Hancock work cited is *Fingerprints of the Gods* (Russian title: *Следы богов*). *Underworld* and *Mirror of Heaven* are each cited once. Hancock functions in Sklyarov's corpus primarily as a **convenient secondary source** — a compiler of physical and ethnographic evidence that Sklyarov adopts while diverging sharply on explanatory framework. The single explicit refutation is Hancock's Orion correlation for Teotihuacan (Book 23). There are no direct engagements with Hancock's ayahuasca / plant-medicine work (*Supernatural*) anywhere in the corpus.

## Key Findings

### 1. The Corpus-Wide Pattern: Data Yes, Framework No

Across 10+ books, Sklyarov consistently adopts Hancock's *Fingerprints of the Gods* as a secondary data source while maintaining a fundamentally different explanatory framework. Hancock is treated as a useful evidence compiler whose conclusions must be independently verified. The synthesis document `SYNTHESIS_people_and_sources.md` explicitly records Sklyarov's stance as "PARTIALLY" — "uses Hancock's synthesis extensively as a secondary source but explicitly refutes Hancock's Orion correlation."

### 2. The One Explicit Refutation

Book 23 (*Pyramids: Construction Mysteries*) contains the sole outright refutation: Hancock's claim that Teotihuacan pyramids reproduce the Belt of Orion in the 11th millennium BCE is checked against StarCal and found to have a ~100% angular discrepancy. Sklyarov's verdict: "no exact reproduction at all." Notably, he does NOT refute the Giza-Orion correlation, which he accepts as approximate.

### 3. The Central Framework Divergence

The deepest divergence is structural rather than factual. Both Sklyarov and Hancock accept:
- A real global catastrophe at ~10,000–12,500 BCE
- Real ancient structures (Giza, Teotihuacan, Yonaguni) that precede accepted dating
- Sphinx water erosion as proof of pre-10,000 BCE construction
- Flood myths as encoding real events
- An advanced pre-catastrophe civilization

They diverge on one question above all others: **Was this civilization human or extraterrestrial?**

- Hancock: Human lost maritime civilization (Atlantis-type), destroyed by the Younger Dryas catastrophe; survivors transmitted knowledge to successor civilizations.
- Sklyarov: Extraterrestrial civilization ("the gods"), physically present on Earth, departed before/during the catastrophe, returned after to build Giza, then gradually withdrew. Human civilizations are their clients/servants/product, not their successors.

### 4. Hancock's Later Work Is Entirely Absent

Sklyarov's corpus does not engage with *Supernatural* (2005), the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis (which Hancock began endorsing ~2007 onward in updated *Fingerprints* editions and public talks), or any of Hancock's work on consciousness, plant medicines, or shamanic states. The temporal mismatch matters: Sklyarov's main books were written between 2000 and 2014; Hancock's intellectual evolution after 2005 is not visible in Sklyarov's citations.

### 5. Books With No Hancock Content

Books 01, 02, 05, 07, 09, 11, 13, 14, 15, 21, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 32 contain no Hancock references. These cover: I Ching mathematics, Spirit Physics theory, the fate of Phaethon, the gods' home planet, the Flood myth calculations (note: Book 10 is the "Flood myth calculations" — with extensive Hancock; the absence list means those other books on different topics do not cite Hancock).
