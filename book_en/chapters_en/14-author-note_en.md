# Author's Note

This book began with a question that would not leave: why did two researchers who agreed on eighty percent of the physical evidence disagree on the most fundamental question the evidence raised?

Graham Hancock and Andrey Sklyarov spent a combined sixty years and wrote a combined forty books examining the same ancient stone walls, the same impossible construction tolerances, the same mythological texts. They reached the same measurements. They drew opposite conclusions about who held the tools. No one had written about the divergence, because no one had systematically compared both corpora. Hancock wrote in English for millions of readers. Sklyarov wrote in Russian for a few thousand. The two bodies of work existed in separate worlds.

The separation ended when the AI-assisted process described in the Introduction made it possible to compare both corpora systematically. The patterns that emerged belong to the evidence. The decision to write about them belongs to the author.

The author is not an archaeologist and not a physicist. He is a person who encountered a question in the gap between two bodies of work that no one else had placed side by side, and who found, in the space between their agreement and their disagreement, something worth a book.

Nothing in these pages argues that Hancock is right, that Sklyarov is right, or that mainstream archaeology is wrong. The argument is simpler: when the evidence runs out, the person continues.
